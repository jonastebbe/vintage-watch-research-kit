# Photos to request when researching a vintage watch

Request only the views needed to resolve your question. Ask for clear, unfiltered images; keep the original files for your private notes.

- [ ] Dial straight on, with printing and minute track in focus.
- [ ] Dial at a slight angle, showing surface texture and reflections.
- [ ] Hands and hour markers close up.
- [ ] Both case sides, including crown and pushers where present.
- [ ] Caseback and its visible inscriptions.
- [ ] Legible reference markings, with their location identifiable.
- [ ] Bracelet end links, clasp and their markings, if relevant.
- [ ] Visible damage, repairs or details mentioned in the description.
- [ ] Dimensions, stating what was measured and whether the crown was included.
- [ ] Movement and inside caseback **only if accessible safely by a qualified person**.
- [ ] Documents relevant to a specific claim, with private information redacted.

Do not ask an inexperienced owner to open a case, remove hands or dismantle a watch. Do not treat photographic appearance alone as proof of material, untouched lume, water resistance or originality.

## Attach a question to each request

Instead of “more photos”, write: “Could you provide a sharp photograph of the reference marking? I cannot read it in the current images.”

Record the answer and its evidence ID in [the worksheet](identification-worksheet.md). For unfamiliar terms, use [The Index's glossary](https://theindex.watch/glossary).
